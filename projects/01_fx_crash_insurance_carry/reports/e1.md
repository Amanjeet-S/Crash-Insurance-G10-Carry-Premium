# E1 and E4: estimation record

This record covers Stage 3 of the [research design](../research_design.md): the construction of the skew price of protection, the samples, the inference and the aggregate results. Month-level series stay in `data/private/results/` under the licence terms.

## Reproduction

```bash
.venv/bin/python scripts/calibrate_smiles.py --start 2010-07-30 --end 2026-08-31
.venv/bin/python scripts/calibrate_smiles.py --contributor FN --start 2007-01-31 --end 2026-08-31
.venv/bin/python scripts/estimate_e1.py
```

## Construction

At each New York month-end the available currencies are ranked by the forward discount fd = log(X/F_X), with X in USD per unit of the currency, and the portfolio is long the three highest and short the three lowest; the extended sample uses two and two. A currency without the calibration set (spot, forward, rate, ATM, 25Δ risk reversal and 25Δ butterfly) is excluded that month, and a month is used only if at least twice the number of legs remain.

Each leg is protected by a one-month option that pays when the leg loses: a put on the currency, which is a USD call for USD-base pairs, struck at the 10Δ strike of the calibrated smile in the pair's delta convention (`src/qef/fx/crash.py` gives the mapping). V_smile and V_flat are the premia at the smile volatility and at the ATM volatility, in USD per USD of forward notional at delivery; for both quotation types this equals the undiscounted premium on the quoted pair divided by the pair forward. The estimand is φ = C_skew / FD, with C_skew = (1/n) Σ (V_smile − V_flat) and FD = (1/n)(Σ_long fd − Σ_short fd).

E1 uses the market-strangle reading and E4 the smile-strangle reading, and 25Δ hedges are a comparison. The ATM comparison is identically zero, because the calibrated smile passes through the ATM volatility. Spot, delivery and expiry dates are built on the New York business calendar (`src/qef/data/smile_inputs.py`), and volatility time is (expiry − trade)/365.

## Samples and inference

The primary sample uses composite quotes with three long and three short legs, from the primary start set by the audit rule to the last complete month. The extended sample uses Fenics quotes before the primary start, with two long and two short legs. The zero-rate regime runs from the primary start to December 2021 and the hiking regime from January 2022.

The pre-registered inference reports regime means with Newey and West (1987) standard errors, with the bandwidth chosen by the automatic procedure of Newey and West (1994) without prewhitening. The difference between the regimes is the coefficient on a regime dummy, with a Newey–West standard error, and its stationary-bootstrap percentile interval (Politis and Romano, 1994) resamples each regime separately with its own automatic block length (Politis and White, 2004; Patton, Politis and White, 2009), using 9,999 draws.

After the first results I added supplementary analyses, recorded in the research log on 24 September 2026; they do not replace the pre-registered estimand. They are the standard error of the difference across Newey–West lags from 0 to 20; an AR(1) plug-in standard error and the effective sample size per regime, T(1 − ρ)/(1 + ρ); medians, 10% trimmed means and the log-φ contrast; the ratio of regime means, mean C_skew over mean FD; separate regime differences for C_skew and FD; and the difference excluding 2020–2021 and excluding the largest zero-rate observations.

The splice test compares the skew cost of the long-side and short-side protective options leg by leg between Fenics and composite quotes, over every currency-month in which both have the calibration set, separately before and within the primary sample.

## Results

The primary sample has 160 month-ends from 31 May 2013 to 31 August 2026, 104 in the zero-rate regime (to December 2021) and 56 in the hiking regime. C_skew and FD are in basis points of notional per month. Newey–West standard errors are in parentheses, and the interval is the stationary-bootstrap 95% percentile interval for the difference (hiking minus zero-rate).

Under the pre-registered rules the regime difference in φ is not claimed, because both intervals include zero under both butterfly readings and both hedge deltas. The butterfly reading changes φ by about 4 per cent (E4 against E1). The C_skew and FD rows split φ into its numerator and denominator; that split was added after the first results and is therefore labelled post hoc.

The post hoc split shows that the skew price of crash insurance per month is essentially the same in the two regimes (difference −0.05 bp, s.e. 1.30), while the forward-discount spread is higher in the hiking regime (difference 8.44 bp, s.e. 2.30, interval [3.49, 13.65]). φ is highly persistent, with first-order autocorrelations of 0.85 and 0.76, which leaves roughly 8 effective observations per regime; an AR(1) plug-in standard error of the difference is 0.27 (t = −1.31). The Newey–West standard error of the difference rises with the lag, from 0.079 at lag 0 to 0.145 at 3, 0.180 at 6, 0.204 at 9, 0.220 at 12, 0.229 at 15 and 0.236 at 20, and the two-sided bootstrap p-value is 0.064.

Other location measures tell the same story. From the zero-rate to the hiking regime the median of φ falls from 0.53 to 0.38 and the 10% trimmed mean from 0.63 to 0.41; the mean of log φ falls from −0.46 to −0.89, a difference of −0.43; and the ratio of regime means, mean C_skew over mean FD, falls from 0.60 to 0.41. The difference depends on a few months. Excluding 2020–2021, the zero-rate months with the smallest forward discounts, it is −0.03 (80 zero-rate months), and dropping the largest 1, 3, 5 and 10 zero-rate observations gives −0.32, −0.28, −0.23 and −0.14.

Fenics and composite skew costs agree leg by leg in the splice test, with correlations of 0.995 before the primary sample and 0.997 within it. The extended sample, 72 Fenics month-ends from January 2007 to April 2013, has a mean C_skew of 22.3 bp and a mean φ of 0.64; because Fenics butterflies before 2010 are often stale, it is secondary evidence.
