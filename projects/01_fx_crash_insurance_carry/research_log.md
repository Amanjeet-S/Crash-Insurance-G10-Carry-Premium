# Research log

## 2026-09-23

I fixed version 1.0 of the design before computing any return, hedge cost or option-implied statistic. Because a three-parameter smile reproduces either butterfly reading exactly, the reading is set by provider documentation and then by the interdealer convention, with the 10Δ fit as supporting evidence only. The primary hedge is at 10Δ, since at one month 25Δ strikes lie only about 2% out of the money. WM fixings are not licensed, so option payoffs use end-of-day spot, with a sensitivity check. For the independent cross-check I chose QuantLib over the MATLAB implementation first considered, because it is open source and reproducible without a licence.

The pricing and smile engine is in place: Garman–Kohlhagen pricing in four delta conventions, delta inversion including the premium-adjusted branch rule, SABR calibration under both butterfly readings and static-arbitrage checks. Its tests cover parity, the delta-neutral straddle, inversion round trips and synthetic SABR recovery under both readings and both delta types. Against QuantLib 1.43, an independent C++ implementation, premiums, deltas in the spot, forward and premium-adjusted conventions, delta-to-strike inversion, delta-neutral ATM strikes and Hagan SABR volatilities agree to between 1e-9 and 1e-13 on the test grid (`tests/test_quantlib_crosscheck.py`).

## 2026-09-24

Matching daily history against intraday bars over windows in January, March and August to September 2026 places the composite daily value at about 21:18 UTC in both summer and winter, a fixed UTC time rather than a New York local close, and the Fenics daily value at about 17:16 London time. Option payoffs and month-end alignment use these times, and the sensitivity check in the design (same-day and previous-day close) is unchanged.

For check 6, the instrument metadata name only the quote type and the delta. LSEG's surface-construction documentation gives defaults for its own surfaces (spot delta, premium-adjusted) but does not define the contributed quotes, so the delta and premium conventions follow market convention (Clark, 2011; Reiswich and Wystup, 2012), as configured. For check 7, provider documentation is silent, and under the pre-registered order of evidence the market-strangle reading is primary. The supporting diagnostic points the other way: across the primary sample, smiles calibrated under the smile-strangle reading predict the held-out 10Δ butterflies better in most month-ends and in every currency, with small absolute differences. I record this as evidence against the primary reading, and E4, which recomputes E1 and the skew term of E3 under the smile-strangle reading, is therefore reported alongside the primary results rather than as a footnote.

In Stage 2 every month-end smile in the primary sample was calibrated under both readings. All converged, with residuals below 1e-13, and all were free of static arbitrage within ±4 and ±10 ATM standard deviations; the counts and diagnostics are in the private results, and Stage 2's completion criterion is met. Calibration exposed a bug: the market-strangle butterfly bracket in `quotes_from_smile` could test a negative strangle volatility for low-volatility pairs, which sent the premium-adjusted delta maximiser into an unbounded loop. Volatility is now validated, bracket expansions are bounded and the butterfly bracket keeps σ_ATM + BF positive, with regression tests added; the delta-to-strike search now brackets locally before scanning and evaluates the smile on the whole grid at once. Stage 1 still needs a second retrieval to check for quote revisions.

I verified the bibliographic details of every cited source (Crossref, OpenAlex, JSTOR, Project Euclid and publisher pages) and checked each specific claim against the text; [references.md](references.md) records what each source is used for and which version was read. The checks led to six corrections. The entropy bound is attributed jointly to Bansal and Lehmann (1997) and Alvarez and Jermann (2005), as stated in Backus, Chernov and Zin (2014). The remark on strict-local-martingale exchange rates now states that the foreign measure exists but is not equivalent (Carr, Fisher and Ruf, 2014). Brandt, Cochrane and Santa-Clara (2006) are cited for a risk-sharing index, not a correlation. Delta conventions are cited to Reiswich and Wystup (2012), whose working-paper version was read, and Clark (2011) only as reported there. The design now uses the full automatic procedure, and no standard error had been computed under the earlier rule.

I estimated E1, E4, the 25Δ comparison, the extended sample and the splice test as specified (`scripts/estimate_e1.py`; method record in [reports/e1.md](reports/e1.md)), holding the results in `data/private/results/` until the licence terms for publishing LSEG-derived findings were confirmed. I added supplementary analyses after the first E1 results, because the series is highly persistent and its zero-rate distribution has a heavy right tail from months with very small forward discounts. ## 2026-09-24 (Stage 4, part A)

The licence holder advised that LSEG data are for individual study and research, may not be redistributed and may not be placed in a public repository, while publishing the extraction code is acceptable. The repository therefore publishes code, methods and aggregate results, and keeps LSEG data, month-level series and calibrated parameters in `data/private/`.

Background sources on which no computation depends were deferred to the report, and [references.md](references.md) marks them.

Two corrections were made before recording. The first implementation of the E2 bootstrap resampled blocks of (return, predictor-innovation) pairs, and adjacent pairs inside a block reintroduced the sample's predictive relation into the null distribution, which inflated the estimated bias about tenfold relative to Stambaugh's approximation; the bootstrap now draws pairs of unrestricted-regression residuals and AR(1) residuals independently, and its bias agrees with the approximation. The E3 reference θ₀ is now the average of the hedges' forward deltas over legs, because the leg weights had summed to two. The design names the Clark–West test without specifying the standard error; Clark and West recommend the least-squares standard error for one-step forecasts, so that is primary and Newey–West is reported alongside.

The aggregate results are in [reports/e1.md](reports/e1.md) and [reports/stage4.md](reports/stage4.md). Under the pre-registered rules the E1 regime difference is not significant, the bias-corrected E2 slope on φ is positive (one-sided p = 0.021) and the out-of-sample Clark–West statistic is 1.640 against a 5% critical value of 1.645, so the regime-shift claim has partial support only. In E3 the realised hedge cost is almost entirely the skew term, and θ_UB is weakly identified. E5 remains, and it needs individual VIX futures contracts rather than the continuation series acquired so far.

## 2026-09-24 (Stage 4, part B: E5)

I took individual VIX futures contracts from Cboe's historical settlement files, one per contract from January 2007 to December 2026, stored privately (`scripts/acquire_cboe_vx.py`). Before 26 March 2007 the archive quotes contracts at ten times the index level; the change of scale is detected in the files themselves and removed, including for contracts that expired before that date, after which Cboe second-month settlements equal the LSEG second-month continuation at every month-end. Caballero and Doyle (2012) short the VIX future whose expiry matches the one-month forward's maturity and hold it to expiry; the design's factor is a month-end analogue, the second-month contract held over the carry return window from the month-end to the FX option expiry.

The data check exposed an error in the Stage 4 return windows before recording: the last E1 month-end's return window ends after the last spot observation, and the spot look-up had substituted an earlier quote. Windows that end after the data are now excluded, and substitution is limited to five New York business days as designed. The primary return sample is 159 months, the E2 and E3 estimates changed only in the third significant figure, and [reports/stage4.md](reports/stage4.md) reports the corrected values.

In E5, unhedged carry loads strongly on the VIX roll-down, and the 10Δ hedge lowers the loading by about a fifth. After adjusting for the VIX and FX-volatility factors neither portfolio earns a significant α, and the exposure-adjusted hedge-cost ratio is unidentified. Stage 4 is complete, and Stage 5 is next.

## 2026-09-24 (Stage 5: proofs)

The remaining results are now proved in `theory/notes.tex`: R3 (spanning, with the integrability condition for taking expectations term by term); R4 (bounds on the unobserved tails from observed wing prices and a tail exponent); R5(c) (monotonicity of the smile delta, and hence injectivity of delta-to-strike inversion); R8 (smoothness of the Hagan volatility for β = 1 and local well-posedness of the smile calibration by the implicit function theorem); and the Garman–Kohlhagen PDE (verification, and uniqueness among solutions of polynomial growth). On the panel, the R5(c) condition holds over ±4 ATM standard deviations at every calibrated month-end, with a maximum of 0.76 against the bound of 1; premium-adjusted deltas are monotone on the conventional branch everywhere; and the Jacobian condition numbers confirm the non-singularity hypothesis of R8 at every calibration.

## 2026-09-24 (Stage 5: choices fixed before the remaining variants)

I recorded these choices before computing any of the estimates they govern. The three-month forward points and rates (USD3MOIS=, USD3MD=, USDSROIS3M= and <CCY>3MD= for the nine currencies) were retrieved on 24 September 2026 into a separate private retrieval folder, while the volatility quotes come from the 23 September retrieval. The three-month variant rebalances quarterly with non-overlapping returns at calendar-quarter month-ends from June 2013, discounts with OIS for USD and deposits otherwise as at one month, holds three long and three short legs on the three-month forward discount with protective options at the 10Δ strike of the three-month market-reading SABR smile, estimates E1, in-sample E2 and E3 as at one month, and reports the two other quarterly phases alongside as supplementary.

The vanna–volga variant places its pillars at the ATM delta-neutral-straddle strike and at the 25Δ put and call strikes at the pillar volatilities in the pair's delta convention. Under the market reading the smile strangle is solved so that the vanna–volga smile prices the market strangle, and under the smile reading the quote is used directly. The primary vanna–volga method is chosen from the source reading before estimation, and months in which a method is undefined at the 10Δ strikes are counted rather than dropped silently.

For the option-implied moments of R4, used as secondary E2 predictors, the moments are those of log(X/F_X) under the USD forward measure, with USD-base prices converted by the change of numeraire of R1. The integration uses the calibrated smile between the 10Δ strikes and the R4 tail bounds beyond them. There are two settings for the tail exponents: (i) the local elasticities of the calibrated smile's density at the 10Δ strikes, which assumes the tails beyond the quotes are no heavier than at the quotes, and (ii) γ = η = 2. The predictors are portfolio variance and oriented skewness averaged over the E1 legs, and the regressions are run at the lower endpoint, midpoint and upper endpoint of each interval under setting (i), with a conclusion reported only if it holds at all three.

## 2026-09-25 (Stage 5: vanna–volga, three-month tenor, moment intervals)

For vanna–volga I read Castagna and Mercurio (2007; the authors' post-review preprint), their working paper (September 2006 revision, compared with the January 2006 version), Bossens et al. (2010; arXiv v3) and Reiswich (2010, doctoral dissertation); Reiswich and Wystup (2012) was checked and does not treat vanna–volga. [references.md](references.md) records the versions and locations. From this reading, and before estimation, I fixed the primary vanna–volga smile as the implied volatility of the vanna–volga price rather than either approximation: the properties Castagna and Mercurio prove belong to the price, the first-order approximation overvalues the wings and the second-order approximation can be undefined, so the approximations are computed as checks. One rule was added in implementation: a 10Δ strike on a vanna–volga smile must be connected to the ATM pillar through strikes where the smile is defined, because the delta map is not continuous across an undefined region. The rule is not from a source, and it does not bind for the price smile's strikes used in the variant.

Each of the three pieces was reviewed separately after implementation, and confirmed defects were fixed before any result was recorded; none of the fixes changed an estimate. In the vanna–volga code, missing diagnostic flags had been counted as passes, the held-out comparison had included non-converged smiles, a citation range was wrong and the strike-set invariance test was missing. In the three-month code, the holiday-table guard did not bound the delivery date and a reproduction command was missing. In the moment code, a closed-form check had been applied outside its stated domain, a test case did not exercise the edge extremum it claimed to, and one docstring statement was wrong.

The vanna–volga and three-month results are in [reports/robustness.md](reports/robustness.md), and the moment predictors in [reports/stage4.md](reports/stage4.md). Vanna–volga smiles leave E1, E2 and E3 unchanged and fit the held-out 10Δ quotes better than SABR. The three-month tenor repeats the one-month pattern: φ falls in the hiking regime because the carry spread widens while the skew price is stable, and the difference is significant under the bootstrap but not under Newey–West. Under γ = η = 2 no skewness interval excludes zero, so truncated quotes do not identify skewness. Under the boundary-elasticity setting, oriented skewness predicts carry returns with the crash-compensation sign under the bootstrap at every endpoint, but that setting is contradicted by the smile's own wings in about three quarters of currency-months, so the result is conditional supporting evidence only.

USD3MOIS= is used as the USD OIS rate at three months, although the 24 September retrieval has no provider metadata confirming its description. What remains is the C++ kernel, the Stage 1 quote-revision check and Stage 6.

## 2026-09-26 (originality audit of the theory)

I checked every place where a result, a proof or a contribution was described as mine against the literature, using the sources recorded in references.md and several read for this purpose. Four results I had marked as proved by me are known results with published or standard proofs, and they are now marked P* and credited. The characterisation of call prices in R2 is Lemma 7.23 of Föllmer and Schied (2004), proved by the same argument. The spanning proof in R3 is the standard Taylor-remainder argument of Carr and Madan. The single maximum of the premium-adjusted call delta in R5(b) is in Jäckel (2020, Section 2), through the same inverse Mills ratio. The decomposition in R7 is that of Backus, Foresi and Telmer (2001, eqs. (9)–(13)), whose published version I read for this audit; Lustig and Verdelhan (2019) restate it with a wedge, and the covered-interest-parity term is only an elementary step. The Garman–Kohlhagen verification is textbook material.

Four results keep a claim, narrowed and set against their closest sources. For R4, Jiang and Tian (2005, Proposition 2) already bound truncation errors of model-free variance; my bounds use a different tail condition and extend to the higher moment contracts. For R5(c), Reiswich (2010) shows that smile deltas can fail to be monotone; the sufficient condition is mine. For R6, the leading-order form is due to Farhi et al.; the exact form and the error bound are mine. For R8, the implicit-function-theorem argument follows Reiswich (2010, Theorems 2 and 3); its application to the three SABR equations is mine. Where I claim a part as my own, I have not found it in the sources I consulted, which is all that can be said without a wider search. The paper's stated contributions are now described as evidence the paper adds, with Bossens et al. (2010) credited for the difference between the two butterfly readings. The notes, the theory README, the paper, the summary and the reference register were changed accordingly.

I then read Fieller (1954), whose pivotal construction for a ratio of means is the test-inversion set the design specifies for θ_UB. The design, the theory README, the paper and the Stage 4 report now credit it. Adding the citation to research_design.md changes no rule or procedure.
