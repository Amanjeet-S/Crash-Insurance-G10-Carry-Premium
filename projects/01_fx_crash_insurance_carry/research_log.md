# Research log

## 2026-09-23

I fixed version 1.0 of the design before computing any return, hedge cost or option-implied statistic. Because a three-parameter smile reproduces either butterfly reading exactly, the reading is set by provider documentation and then by the interdealer convention, with the 10Δ fit as supporting evidence only. The primary hedge is at 10Δ, since at one month 25Δ strikes lie only about 2% out of the money. WM fixings are not licensed, so option payoffs use end-of-day spot, with a sensitivity check. For the independent cross-check I chose QuantLib over the MATLAB implementation first considered, because it is open source and reproducible without a licence.

The pricing and smile engine is in place: Garman–Kohlhagen pricing in four delta conventions, delta inversion including the premium-adjusted branch rule, SABR calibration under both butterfly readings and static-arbitrage checks. Its tests cover parity, the delta-neutral straddle, inversion round trips and synthetic SABR recovery under both readings and both delta types. Against QuantLib 1.43, an independent C++ implementation, premiums, deltas in the spot, forward and premium-adjusted conventions, delta-to-strike inversion, delta-neutral ATM strikes and Hagan SABR volatilities agree to between 1e-9 and 1e-13 on the test grid (`tests/test_quantlib_crosscheck.py`).

Stage 1 still needs a second retrieval to check for quote revisions.

## 2026-09-24 (Stage 5: choices fixed before the remaining variants)

I recorded these choices before computing any of the estimates they govern. The three-month forward points and rates (USD3MOIS=, USD3MD=, USDSROIS3M= and <CCY>3MD= for the nine currencies) were retrieved on 24 September 2026 into a separate private retrieval folder, while the volatility quotes come from the 23 September retrieval. The three-month variant rebalances quarterly with non-overlapping returns at calendar-quarter month-ends from June 2013, discounts with OIS for USD and deposits otherwise as at one month, holds three long and three short legs on the three-month forward discount with protective options at the 10Δ strike of the three-month market-reading SABR smile, estimates E1, in-sample E2 and E3 as at one month, and reports the two other quarterly phases alongside as supplementary.

## 2026-09-27 (identification of option-implied moments, model validation and the regime comparison)

The secondary E2 results left a gap. Under setting (ii) no skewness interval excludes zero, and setting (i) is contradicted by the smile's own wings, so the reports could say only that skewness is weakly identified. I set out to make that statement precise, and every analysis in this entry is post hoc.

I first proved R10 (theory notes and `theory/README.md`). Under the hypotheses of R4, an exponent is compatible with the boundary price and slope of the smile exactly when it is at most γ̄ = K_min G0/P0 − 1 in the lower tail and η̄ = 1 + K_max Ḡ0/C0 in the upper tail; at those exponents the tails are unique power laws. Representing the lower tail as G0 z^γ times the distribution function of a mixing measure turns the identified set of the moment contracts into a moment problem over that measure. This shows that R4's upper bounds are attained, that its lower bounds of zero are not sharp, and that the joint identified set of the three raw moments, not the box of R4, is what decides the sign of the skewness. It also gives a check of setting (i), γ_i ≤ γ̄, and a breakdown point θ* for the sign of the skewness along the path of exponents θ γ̄ and 1 + θ(η̄ − 1).

Before claiming any of this, I searched the literature for each part. The search corrected one claim of mine: I had described the tail hypothesis as a local form of the generalised unimodality of Olshen and Savage (1970), but their condition runs the other way, and the correct frame is the reversed hazard rate (star) order against the power law (Lando, Arab and Oliveira, 2022). I withdrew the claim before it entered any document. The search also found that the exponent K P'/P at the last strike is proposed as a natural extrapolation exponent by Benaim, Dodgson and Kainth (2008), that Qin, Yang, Che and Feng (2026) construct the matching power-law tails, and that the reduction to a moment problem and its two-point extreme points are classical (Kemperman, 1968; Winkler, 1988; Popescu, 2005). R10 credits all of these, and my claim is limited to the admissibility characterisation with uniqueness in the whole class, the generating family with the boundary value fixed and its consequences for R4, the check of setting (i), and the breakdown analysis. A second proposal, an attribution identity for the change in the carry premium, turned out to be the hedged-versus-unhedged accounting of Burnside et al. (2011), Jurek (2014) and Farhi et al. (2015), so I do not claim it.

I implemented the identified sets in `src/qef/fx/identification.py`, restricting the mixing measures to a graded grid of step points and solving the linear programmes with HiGHS through highspy 1.15.1, which I added to the locked requirements; `tests/test_identification.py` checks the admissible exponents, the Pareto point, the sharpness of R4's upper bound, the closed-form lower bound, containment of the lognormal truth, nesting and grid refinement. An earlier version lost all precision near the boundary, because it differenced cumulative integrals taken from the far end of the tail; the tests caught this, and the integrals are now accumulated from the boundary outwards.

For the model validation I used Merton (1976) and Heston (1993) models with one-month FX-typical parameters, priced in QuantLib (Merton through its Bates engine with constant variance, checked against eq. (19) of Merton's paper, and Heston through the analytic engine, checked against direct Fourier prices), with exact moments from the cumulant generating functions and exact tail rates. I checked every numerical statement in the module's documentation against a fresh run before using it. In all eight model and pair combinations the untruncated spanning reproduces the model moments, and both the R4 intervals and the R10 identified sets contain the true moments at the model's own tail exponents. The models' own exponents are between 0.58 and 0.97 of the maximal ones; setting (i) fails in seven of the eight cases, and the sign of the skewness is identified at the model's own exponents whenever the skewness is not close to zero, with θ* between 0.21 and 0.56 (`reports/model_validation.md`).

Finally I compared the regime changes in the skew price and in the forward-discount spread directly (`scripts/estimate_regime_attribution.py`, reported in `reports/e1.md`). The skew price did not change (−0.05 bp, s.e. 1.30) and neither did the oriented 10Δ risk reversal (0.21 vol points, s.e. 0.29), while the spread rose by 8.44 bp, which remains significant with a persistence-robust standard error (t = 2.80). The ratio of the two changes, Wald's grouping estimator, is −0.006 with a 95% test-inversion set of [−0.26, 0.49], and a one-for-one rise is rejected (t = −2.66). Read through the equations of Farhi and Gabaix (2016), this says that the rise in the spread did not come from disaster exposure; that reading is mine and rests on treating the spread as an ex-ante premium.

I then applied R10 to all 1,440 one-month currency-months of the primary sample (`scripts/estimate_identification.py`, reported in `reports/stage4.md`). The largest compatible exponents have medians 59.7 (lower tail) and 67.8 (upper tail). Setting (i) exceeds them in one or both tails in 75.3% of currency-months, and every currency-month refuted this way also fails the smile-extrapolation diagnostic I recorded on 25 September, which confirms that finding with a check that uses only the boundary price, slope and curvature. With the sharp identified set, the sign of the skewness is identified in 93.3% of the currency-months where setting (i) is admissible, in none under setting (ii), and in 26.7%, 70.3% and 87.6% at half, three quarters and nine tenths of the maximal exponents. The breakdown point θ* has quartiles 0.49, 0.63 and 0.80. A fourfold refinement of the step grid in 40 currency-months moved no bound by more than 1.2×10⁻³ of its interval width and changed no θ*. The first attempt ran slowly because each worker process used a multithreaded BLAS, which oversubscribed the machine; limiting each worker to one thread fixed it.

My conclusion from the three analyses is that one-month option-implied skewness from quotes that stop at the 10Δ strikes is identified only under a strong and explicit tail assumption, which standard models sometimes meet and sometimes do not, and that the rise in the carry spread between the regimes was not accompanied by any rise in the price of crash insurance. Both findings are post hoc, and the paper, the summary, the reports and the theory documents label them so.

While checking the new numbers against the stored outputs I found a rounding error in an earlier report: the lower end of the bootstrap interval for the regime difference in the forward-discount spread is 3.48485 bp, which I had reported as 3.49; the paper and the E1 record now give 3.48. No other bootstrap bound in the E1 tables was affected.

## 2026-09-27 (plan, fixed before retrieval: out-of-sample test of the tail hypotheses with 5Δ quotes)

The identification results rest on hypotheses about the tails beyond the 10Δ strikes that the quotes used so far cannot test. LSEG carries one-month 5Δ risk reversals and butterflies from the Fenics contributor for all nine currencies. So far I have seen only their coverage: two-sided daily quotes from June 2022 to September 2026, about fifty month-ends per currency, all in the hiking regime. They have never entered any calibration, so they can test the hypotheses out of sample. I record the test before retrieving the history.

Data. `<CCY>1MR5=FN` and `<CCY>1MB5=FN` for the nine currencies, added to the instrument catalogue and retrieved into a new retrieval folder with the same requested range as before. Month-ends follow the month-end rule of the design (section 4). The sample is every month-end from July 2022 to August 2026 with a converged Fenics market-reading smile and both 5Δ quotes observed or substituted. The boundary is the Fenics smile, already calibrated to Fenics ATM, 25Δ risk reversal and 25Δ butterfly quotes, so that quotes and smile come from one contributor, with 10Δ strikes in each pair's convention as in the moment analysis.

Conversion. The 5Δ put and call volatilities are σ_ATM + BF5 − RR5/2 and σ_ATM + BF5 + RR5/2 (the smile-strangle reading, with the risk reversal as call minus put), with σ_ATM the Fenics ATM mid. The strikes follow from the flat-volatility delta inversion in each pair's convention (R5), and the out-of-the-money prices are undiscounted Garman–Kohlhagen premia. Under the market-strangle reading the two quotes do not determine the two volatilities without a smile; the smile-strangle reading is also the one that predicted the held-out 10Δ quotes better (smile calibration report). As a check of semantics, I record the sign agreement of RR5 with RR10.

Tests. Under H_L(γ), with the boundary price and slope P0, G0 at K_min, the identified interval of the put price at K < K_min is [max(0, P0 − G0 K_min(1 − k^{γ+1})/(γ+1)), P0 k^{γ+1}] with k = K/K_min; under H_U(η) the call price at K > K_max has [max(0, C0 − Ḡ0 K_max(1 − k^{1−η})/(η − 1)), C0 k^{1−η}] with k = K/K_max. Both follow from the representation in R10, and the upper ends are R4's bounds. For each currency-month and tail I compute (i) whether the 5Δ price lies in the interval as the exponent tends to its lower limit, which is the weakest hypothesis of the class; (ii) the largest exponent at which it lies in the interval, as a fraction θ_5 of the maximal exponent (γ̄, or η̄ − 1 with η − 1); and (iii) the sharp identified set of the variance and third central moment with the 5Δ prices added as equality constraints, at the largest exponents the 5Δ prices allow, and the breakdown point of the sign of skewness with this information.

Reporting and interpretation. I will report the distributions of θ_5 in each tail, the share of currency-months in which the 5Δ quotes are inconsistent with the whole class, the share of currency-months in which the sign of skewness is identified at the largest exponents the 5Δ quotes allow, and the breakdown point with the 5Δ information. Consistency with one extra price is necessary for a hypothesis, not sufficient, so identification at the largest allowed exponents is the most favourable case. If the sign is not identified there, it is not identified under any hypothesis of this class consistent with the 5Δ quotes, and I will say so. If it is identified there, I will say only that the 5Δ quotes do not rule identification out. The sample is short and lies in one regime, the 5Δ quotes are Fenics indications rather than trades, and all results are post hoc with respect to the original design.

## 2026-09-27 (independent check of the bootstrap with arch)

The stationary bootstrap and its block-length rule, on which every bootstrap interval in the project rests, were the one core numerical component without an independent implementation to compare with; I had only read the arch package to take its tuning caps. ## 2026-09-28 (independent review of these items, corrections and results)

An independent review of the three items, with each substantive finding checked by a second reviewer who tried to refute it, preceded their reporting. No estimate changed. The corrections were these.

- Long sample: a test-inversion set is unbounded on both sides when the unhedged t-statistic is below 1.96, because the automatic bandwidth does not depend on the scale of the series, so a finite end at the edge of the scanned grid is an artefact. The 2009 to 2021 set had been printed with a finite lower end of −8.49; it is unbounded. The script now decides boundedness from this limit and stops if a bounded set reaches the grid edge. Its docstring had said that I fixed the rules before seeing any return of the sample; it now says which months were known. Where the unhedged mean is negative (2008), a positive θ_UB means that the hedge raised the mean return, and the summary says so. For dollar-base legs the hedge ratio in dollar returns is the premium-adjusted forward delta, so the design's pips form of θ₀ is a leading-order approximation; the premium-adjusted form raises θ₀ by 0.002 to 0.006 and changes no conclusion.
- Design checks: the test of a zero hedge cost (θ_UB = 0) at 10Δ gives t = −2.35 under the normal reference, but the net option profit has skewness 3.6, and a studentised stationary bootstrap (9,999 draws, seed 20260924, the project's block-length rule, the Newey–West standard error recomputed at the automatic lag in each draw), added after the review, gives the interval [−16.4, 1.7] bp, which contains zero. I therefore do not claim that the realised hedge cost differs from zero. The 10Δ set retains θ₀ only at Newey–West lags of 6 or more. The explanation of why the delta method misleads was corrected (the standard error under a tested value is not linear in it near θ_UB), the delta-method t-statistic is oriented as the test-inversion one, and the docstring's statement that all rules were fixed before any result now excepts the post hoc items. These changes are recorded as amendments A1 and A2 in the script.
- Kernel: the inverse normal was wrong for subnormal probabilities and now uses the asymptotic tail expansion, with a remainder bound derived in the source; the bracket check tested the product of the end values, which underflows when both are tiny, and now compares signs after testing for zeros, as brentq does; the density is formed as scipy forms it; a stated difference of 13 units in the last place between the two implementations of Φ was replaced by measured figures (about 20); two stated test rules that were not asserted everywhere are now asserted; and the middle-part wrapper handles non-integer arguments as the reference does. Both implementations lose accuracy in the maximiser of the premium-adjusted call delta like the fourth power of σ√τ, which is harmless here because σ√τ is below 0.3. The benchmark was rerun after the changes.

Results, in the paper and the reports. Over 1995 to 2026 (379 months) unhedged carry earns 37.2 bp per month (t = 2.83) and ATM-hedged carry 16.3 bp, θ_UB is 0.56 and its set is bounded, [0.27, 0.94], but contains the ATM diffusive null of about one half; an ATM hedge has no skew term, so the sample is uninformative about priced crash risk. My reading of what this implies for the 10Δ hedge: the set is bounded once the unhedged t-statistic exceeds 1.96, which at the primary sample's ratio of mean to long-run standard deviation takes about 159 × (1.96/1.47)² ≈ 280 months, while the licensed panel's one-month risk reversals begin in 2007. SOFR discounting changes no E1 conclusion, the named broker's quotes move the skew price by 0.03 bp, the moment code agrees with the closed-form bounds to 1.6e-15, and the kernel agrees with the Python code to 1e-13 relative and is faster by factors from about 1.2 (vectorised code) to about 350 (strike inversion). The paper's list of items not reported is replaced by a paragraph on these items. Hall (1992) is added to the references for the studentised bootstrap, on its bibliographic record and chapter titles.
